# Progress notes — Red Lines (Middle East 2026)

_Last updated: 2026-10-12 (session 5)._

## What exists
A playable browser prototype. No build step: plain ES modules served statically.

```
npm install          # dev deps only (map baking + Playwright)
npm run serve        # http://localhost:8080  (or: node tests/server.mjs 8080)
npm test             # engine checks + situation content probe + browser checks (Playwright/Chromium)
```

### Game loop (session 2 redesign)
Turn = 2 weeks from 12 Oct 2026, 16 turns. One screen per turn:
1. **Situations.** The director deals up to two cards (a crisis and an opportunity, chosen by priority from everything whose `when` is true, honouring cooldowns/`once`) plus an intelligence card on the one AI declaration that matters most to you. Each card has 2–4 options with cost, worded odds ("Likely"), and a fold-out of the reasons. Unanswered cards take their default (always a free option).
2. **Initiative.** One catalogue move. Advisers propose the top five by expected gain (housekeeping moves excluded), with the objective each one advances; the full catalogue is behind a toggle. The preview includes the lobbying the move is expected to meet.
3. **End turn.** Card responses resolve first, then every declaration in random order (AI actors declared before the player moved). Rolls are hidden; outcomes are narrated. Upkeep, events, collapse checks. A briefing overlay shows: your decisions with their measured impact on your tracks, AI moves and events that moved your position (relevance = goal-score delta, resource delta, or touching you/your theaters), and everything else folded away.

Chains: results go into `s.recent` (last 4 turns) and flags; situations read them (`h.did`, `h.hit`, `h.event`, `h.chose`, `h.flag`). Options built from catalogue actions are also recorded as actions, so the AI's grudges and the chains see them (bomb Iran → "Iran answers in the Gulf" + "Jerusalem asks for the big bombs" next turn).

Victory: score ≥ max(75, start+28) at turn 16; draw if ≥ start−5; all objectives met after turn 6 = decisive early win; stability 0 = collapse. (An objectives-met requirement was tried and dropped: scores of 90 ending in a draw felt wrong.)

### Visual pass (session 2, later)
Less prose, more signal: every option/proposal/result has an icon (`src/ui/icons.js`, inline SVG sheet injected at boot; action → icon map plus word heuristics for hand-written options), a five-pip odds meter with the band word, and **effect chips** computed by the engine (`director.effectChips`: simulate the success outcome on a clone, diff the player's tracks/resources/relations/influence, color by whether the change helps the player's objectives). Cost is icon+number. Card briefs show the first sentence with the rest behind "more"; an option's description shows only when selected. Theater markers carry a badge for the card dealt there (! crisis, ✦ opportunity, ◉ intel). The briefing uses outcome glyphs, chips and a score sparkline. Start/help screens cut to a few lines.

### Consequences of force (session 3)
Playtest finding: an all-force US nearly won. Added systemic costs that every actor pays (`src/engine/consequences.js`, hooked into `applyOutcome`, so the AI's lookahead sees them too):
- **Legitimacy**: using force costs regard with every third party not hostile to the victim, scaled by the target (civilian-dense and capital targets, massive bombing, assassinations cost most; EU, Turkey, Egypt object loudest; allies discount 40%).
- **Rally round the flag**: a standing regime whose capital is bombed gains a little stability.
- **Home front**: every American sortie costs war support; war support below 40 erodes US stability faster (÷15); below 25 US diplomacy negotiates "from visible weakness".
- **Fatigue**: consecutive turns of force (tracked per actor in `s.fatigue`) lower the odds of further force (−5%/turn, cap −20%) and cause attrition; forces regenerate at most +3/turn.
- **Escalation grind** (upkeep, war intensity ≥ 60, doubled ≥ 80): US support, fleet and money drain; Gulf, Israel, Iran and EU stability erode; oil adj rises; the Axis mobilizes (+military); ISIS grows; and **Iran reconstitutes** its program (+1–2 breakout/turn while its regime stands). Deployments cost the US money while intensity ≥ 50.
- **Shipping cannot be bombed open**: strikes on Hormuz degrade IRGC coastal influence (raising the traffic cap) but traffic itself recovers only +4/turn, and the cap is reduced up to 45% while the war is hot. Massive bombing of the nuclear complex now removes 30% of the remaining program (min 6), not a flat 25.
- **Bluster**: options matching demand/defy/double down/unconditional/ultimatum/blame/… set a flag; the actor's diplomatic odds are −10% for two turns ("Recent threats poisoned the channel").
- **AI**: a bombed Iran leans toward the dash (lesson of Libya); partners of a struck actor lean toward hitting the attacker (solidarity); grudges unchanged.
- US treasury 100 (was 120), income 11 (was 14).

### Policy simulator (session 4)
Direction from the playtest: make it a policy simulator, where every actor is self-interested, picks moves probabilistically, and the player sees which end states are likely and how each move narrows them.

**Mixed strategies (`src/engine/ai.js`).** `scoreCandidates` evaluates every affordable (action, target) once at commit 0 by one-ply lookahead and records what each outcome does (a sparse diff of tracks, resources, influence, relations); higher commitment levels are scored in closed form from the same two outcomes. `rootPolicy` = softmax (T 0.6) over the top-k distinct moves — an actor's strategy is a distribution, and `sampleMove` draws from it. `fastPolicy` re-scores the recorded moves on any later board through a read-only overlay (no cloning), so simulated actors adapt as the game moves on (once Hormuz is open, escorting is worth nothing; once hit, retaliation rises) at ~1.3 ms per turn for all 19 actors.

**Rollouts (`src/engine/rollout.js`).** `rolloutTurn` plays one turn on a clone: the player's seat by orders, by holding, or by the AI (cards dealt and answered); everyone else samples its strategy. Common random numbers: the clone reseeds at fixed points of every turn from (seed, turn), so two rollouts that differ in one move still see the same draws, rolls and events wherever their boards agree. `improvePolicies` tests each actor's top-k candidates in `rollouts` short games (H 2) in which the others respond (actors hit last turn re-plan), then nudges the one-ply gains by the anchored, clamped rollout advantage (anchor 0.25, clamp ±4). Rollout values are chaotic (one roll flips a war; across seeds the same candidate's value moved 10–20 points), which is why they nudge rather than replace: unanchored, the policies' total variation across seeds was 0.3–0.4; anchored 0.16. The browser runs rollouts 6 × horizon 2 (`?ai=shallow` for one-ply); Node tests run one-ply unless they set `setAIConfig`.

**Forecasts.** `createForecast(s, plan, { n, player })` plays n games to turn 16 from the current board, one per `step()` so the UI time-slices it, and `summarize` gives win/draw/defeat odds, score bands per turn (p10/p25/p50/p75/p90), final-score spread, recurring end states (`END_STATES`, colored by the player's objectives), each objective's likely final value and how often it is met, and who reaches their victory bar. The same seed is shared across the "orders" and "hold" variants (paired comparison). Simulations never touch the main game (LIVE card cache keyed by simulation tag; no effect previews inside sims; option closures verified not to capture the dealing state: 3,247 options checked).

**UI.** Futures tab (outcome bar; fan chart with the past line, 8-of-10 band, median, the holding median dashed, victory/defeat lines; likely end states; objectives at the end with now/median/range; who gets what they want; a cone of how the forecast narrowed turn by turn; how it is computed). Status bar: an Outlook gauge (click → Futures). Orders bar: outlook under the current orders with ▲/▼ win and loss vs holding. Briefing: outlook line patched in place when the new turn's forecast lands (win % was → is, final-score range was → is). Intel: assessed intentions, each actor's two likeliest moves with their probabilities. `holding` is computed once per turn, `orders` whenever the queued orders change (debounced 200 ms, 20 ms slices, cancelled on change). `?noforecast` disables it; `?runs=N` sets the game count (default 20).

Cost (Node): one-ply plan for 19 actors 160 ms; rollout improvement (6×2) ~200 ms; a 20-game forecast ~0.8 s (Chromium: 680 ms); turn start with deep AI ~0.5 s (shallow ~0.25 s). Deep vs shallow AI in the player's seat, 3 seeds each (mean score, W/D/L): US 74 (1/2/0) → 79 (2/1/0); Israel 54 → 51; Iran 85 → 75; Turkey 70 (1/2/0) → 83 (3/0/0); Hezbollah 79 → 82. Not a clear strength gain (the nudge is deliberately small); the point is that moves are tested against everyone's replies.

Design decision (user call): for the US, "war support collapses" (<25) in 65–100% of futures whatever the orders. Kept as is: domestic support eroding through a long war is realistic, and the home front is meant to be the US player's binding constraint. "ISIS resurgent" in 55–75% of futures is the same kind of signal (chaos feeds it) and also stays.

### Operations: orders that take weeks (session 5)
Playtest direction: actions should span turns, tie up forces for their duration, and follow real timelines (a strike with assets in position is a fortnight's work; moving a carrier takes weeks; building up forces takes months).

`src/engine/ops.js`. An action with `op` is an operation:
- **project** – prepares for `duration` turns, then resolves once with a hidden roll (talks 2 turns, arms deliveries 2, a targeted killing 2, annexation 2, mobilization 2, disarmament and normalization talks 3, investment 3, rearmament 3, the nuclear dash 3, a state build-up 4 with +12 to the force ceiling);
- **sustained** – runs fortnight after fortnight, a roll and a per-turn effect each time, for up to `maxTurns` or until stood down (convoy escorts, interdiction campaigns, a 3-turn ground campaign);
- **deploy** – arrives after `duration` turns and stays on station granting a **posture** until stood down (Reinforce CENTCOM: a second carrier group and bomber task force, 3 turns, 20 forces committed, $3 a fortnight; an ISF contingent).
Operations commit forces (`lock`: not spendable while the operation lives; the actor card shows available/total and a hatched segment) and pay `upkeep` every turn after the launch turn; a project that cannot pay stalls, a campaign or deployment that cannot pay stands down. `requires` gates actions on postures: the massive bombing campaign needs the surge on station ("Reinforce CENTCOM first (6 weeks)"), strikes and escorts get +10% with it. Preparing operations are visible to everyone (Intel tab, dashed rings on the map, the popover and tooltip), the intelligence card can report a rival's operation and let you lobby its completion odds, and fragile operations are set back a fortnight by attacks on their site (two by heavy attacks); the nuclear dash is wrecked outright by a massive bombing of the sites. The player stands operations down from the Command tab (an order, not the initiative). AI actors review their campaigns each turn and stand down the ones that no longer pay.

AI: candidates exclude blocked actions; operations are valued as if they went to plan, discounted 0.92 per turn of duration, less a charge for the forces they tie up; a rival's preparing operation that would hurt an actor makes hitting its site worth more (strike the dash before it lands). Rollouts and forecasts tick operations like the real turn, so the futures account for what is already under way. Card options built from operations launch them (no roll at card time; the pips show completion odds, the clock chip the time) and are blocked by the same prerequisites.

Costs: the one-ply plan is ~10% dearer (prerequisite checks), a 20-game forecast ~1.2 s in Chromium.
